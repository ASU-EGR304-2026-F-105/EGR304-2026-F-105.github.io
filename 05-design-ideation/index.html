    Intro/overview

    In this section we are going over the 100 ideas that we have generated based off of the user needs found from the previous sections. We then have sorted and ranked these ideas into different categories and created three unique products based off of these generated ideas. For more information about how we generated our ideas and who helped to generate them, please refer to the very bottom under Step 5 for more information.

    Step Four

    Concept One

    Concept one is a fairly traditional off grid GPS communication device. It has large buttons that are easy to press even in harsh conditions, an offline compass and gps location tracking for consistent reliable position tracking. It also has an easy to read message delivery confirmation section, so that users can feel confident that their messages are getting through to their loved ones. Its made with rugged materials, great for surviving in outdoors environments for long periods of time, with a long battery life to back it up.

    Concept Two

    Concept two is intended to allow a device to work off grid, with an antenna to ensure consistent connectivity, and an OLED display that at a quick glance shows the quality of the connection. This is then easily connected to either the side of a laptop, or the back of a phone, making it easy to connect to any device with the included usb-c cable.

    Step Five

    There were a few key things we wanted to focus on with our brainstorming session. The first of these was making sure that every member was present and participating in the actual brainstorming. To collect the data we decided to continue using the shared Google Doc that we have been working on for the past assignments. This Doc allows multiple people to be working simultaneously without workflow limitations of the GitHub website. Primarily the Doc ensures that work isn’t being done twice, as if we aren’t in person or sharing our screens, it becomes hard to tell what another group member is working on without very detailed communication that tends to distract from the work.

    As we had some members with busier schedules than normal, we weren’t able to meet in person to do the brainstorming, and instead went back to a Discord meeting. This made it so that members who lived further away from campus could work for longer rather than have to drive, work for a bit, and drive back. This ensured that all three of our group members had an equal amount of participation in the idea generation, sorting, and prototype sketching sections of this project. When coming up with our ideas, the main place we started from was two most recent projects, being product requirements and user needs and benchmarking. Specifically user needs and benchmarking gave us a good place to start with the list of 100 user needs. As we obviously couldn’t fit a feature that was representative of every single user need, we instead used the broader categories that we created, stuff like durability, utility, power, control, and accessibility. From there we filled in the categories in some amount of an order, trying to keep like ideas grouped with other like ideas, as it made the most sense for our brains that way. We also figured that this would to some extent simplify the organization process that had to come after the generation phase.

    When it came time to group the data, we mostly based it off of the requirement/need section that was already in existence for the generating ideas section. However, we simplified some of the groups, and combined them to create more logical categories. We also ranked inside of these categories as we figured it would help with the next step of the process, being to generate three concept products that all fit different needs inside of their design. Giving a ranking makes it easier to discuss and create three unique feeling products that share a minimal amount of the user needs.

    The process we used to generate our three concepts might be a bit different then the intended method. We obviously looked again at all of our generated ideas, but we more specifically thought about the products that we had already seen through the voice of the customer benchmarking. This allowed us to create three unique feeling ideas that we could see existing in an actual market. From there we sorted the majority of our generated ideas into these three products, to make them less generalized and guarantee the uniqueness of all three.
